## Overall Impression

This is a product with real ideas and uneven follow-through. The track-colour system, the rotating week and the phone's digest are inventions nobody would arrive at by default. What lets them down is that decisions get *made and written down* and then applied to one layer: the type scale reached the informational pages but not the app until this session; the uppercase ban reached panel headings but not panel interiors; `.tap-44` reached panel chrome but not panel contents; the undo policy reached the phone and not the web.

The single biggest opportunity is not a new feature. It is that there is no DESIGN.md, so every decision lives in a comment attached to the one selector that implements it — and a rule written inside `.section-title` cannot bind `MaterialsTracker`.

## What's Working

**The type scale is real and now enforced.** Eight distinct sizes on the rendered dashboard, every one a token, zero strays — down from forty. Both apps share the vocabulary deliberately so "the subhead" means one thing across web and native.

**The error-recovery philosophy on the phone is argued, not defaulted.** `UndoBar.tsx` opens with the reasoning: a dialog in front of every destructive action teaches people to dismiss dialogs, so the action happens immediately and a bar offers it back — restoring a whole previous workspace rather than inverting the operation, "which is both simpler and actually correct." `ConfirmSheet.tsx` then explains why its two exceptions are exceptions.

**Keyboard focus is genuinely solid.** 17 real Tab presses: no trap, no control focusable without an indicator, and the hover-revealed panel chrome correctly becomes visible on focus.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The web app has no undo and no confirmation; the phone app has both.**
`PortfolioTracker.tsx:172` deletes a deliverable on one click of a 14×14 red ×. Materials and to-dos are the same. There is no undo anywhere in the web tree. The phone's documented reason for *not* confirming is "that is what the undo bar is for" — a rationale the web inherited without the mechanism, so it has neither guard. Local-first, no server history: a mis-click is permanent, on the smallest target in the app.
*Fix:* port `UndoBar` as a provider around `Dashboard.tsx` on the same previous-workspace-snapshot basis (`commitTrack` already takes a producer). Until then, give the three bare × buttons the inline two-step `TrackSettingsModal` already uses. → `$impeccable harden`

**[P0] 49 of 78 interactive elements fail 44×44 at phone width; 35 fail 24×24.**
Measured twice independently with a coarse pointer emulated. `.tap-44` works perfectly on the 24 elements that carry it — a 28×28 control resolves to 44×44, the 136×20 track switcher to 136×44 — and **none of the 49 failures carries it**. The utility went on panel chrome and never reached panel contents. Smallest: 14×14 "Delete project", which is also the P0 above. **My own 0.110.0 report said 22 under 44×44; that was measured on a near-empty dashboard and is wrong for a populated one.**
*Fix:* apply `.tap-44` to the row-level controls in `MaterialsTracker` (Notes/Edit/Archive/find), `PortfolioTracker` (delete, edit), `TodoList`, and the day-card header rows. → `$impeccable adapt`

**[P1] The focus ring fails WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 in both themes.**
`--ring` is `rgba(14,165,233,0.45)`. Composited: 2.23 over the page and 2.27 over a panel in dark; 2.03 / 2.05 in light. Requirement is 3.0. The 0.45 alpha is the direct cause — the same hue at full opacity passes comfortably. Independently verified.
*Fix:* raise the ring's alpha, or give it its own opaque token rather than deriving it from the accent at 45%. → `$impeccable audit`

**[P1] "New track" has no Create button; the template list is the irreversible submit.**
The user names the track, picks a colour and two dates, then meets helper copy saying the next choice "only decides which modules the track opens with… nothing is locked away by choosing here" — above four rows whose `onClick` calls `create()` and closes the dialog. Clicking a row to *read* what "Work" sets up creates a track. It is the primary creation flow and the one place where the interaction contradicts its own adjacent sentence.
*Fix:* make the rows a radio group with a selected state and add a `btn-solid` **Create track** beside Cancel. The helper sentence becomes true once the row is a selection. → `$impeccable harden`

**[P1] The phone app's front door is a blank screen reading "No track open."**
Three words centred in an empty screen: no illustration, no explanation, no button. `(tabs)/today.tsx` three taps away has the model answer — what is missing, what happens when you act, one filled primary button. `(tabs)/progress.tsx` has a third standard: full chrome around one grey square, with the legend 900px below it. Three empty-state standards in a four-tab app, and the worst one is first.
*Fix:* give `index.tsx` the `today.tsx` treatment and suppress Progress's chrome until there is a first active day. Seed new tracks with differentiated phase placeholders instead of "Describe what this phase is about." four times. → `$impeccable onboard`

**[P2] 14 text-contrast failures on the dashboard, identical in both themes.**
Ratios 1.94–2.78 against 4.5, from two causes: `.day-card.opacity-50` on future days (8 nodes — "Fri", "11-06", "Free", "Review") and `text-muted-foreground/60` captions, some nested under a further `.opacity-70` (6 nodes — "Ends", "27 Nov 2026", "· day 23"). Both are deliberate de-emphasis implemented as opacity, which multiplies against an already-quiet token. `/landing` and `/privacy` are clean in both themes.
*Fix:* express "future" and "secondary" as colour tokens that were measured, not as opacity on top of tokens that were. → `$impeccable audit`

## Persona Red Flags

Taps the tab bar because it is the only thing alive; Today, Plan and Progress are equally bare, and Progress shows her a Less▢▢▢▢▢More legend for a grid with one square in it. One click and eight months of record is gone: no confirm, no undo, no toast. He opens the "···" looking for history; it has 15 rows and none of them is history.

## Questions to Consider

1. The phone decided what a module looks like when summarised; the web never had to. What if the web dashboard's default were the phone's digest — nine summary cards with drill-down — and today's full panels were what you get when you open one? That answers the tablet, the PWA, and the 78-controls-on-one-screen problem at once.
2. Nothing in this interface moves the way time moves. The thesis is distance covered over months; the motion vocabulary is a 3% press scale and a staggered fade — the vocabulary of a settings page. What does *approaching a horizon* look like?
3. Two thirds of dashboard text is 11.2px or smaller and the scale has ten steps, two of which the dashboard never reaches. Is the dense panel the right shape for something looked at for eleven seconds a day?
4. `UndoBar.tsx`'s header comment is the best design writing in the repository and it binds nothing outside `mobile/src/`. With no DESIGN.md, what would make a decision like that binding across surfaces?
5. Ticking today's box is the only thing most users do most days, and it produces a 240ms checkbox pop while the grid that is the whole point sits inches away and does not react. If people remember the end, what should the end of a Horizon session feel like — and why is the app's loudest animation its page load?
